# Clinical Guideline: MRI of the Knee for Osteoarthritis

**Guideline ID:** CG-MSK-073721-001
**Last reviewed:** 2026-01-15
**Status:** Synthetic example for ClaimSight portfolio project. Not real clinical advice.

## Summary

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the knee (CPT 73721) is generally considered medically necessary in adult patients with osteoarthritis (ICD-10 M17.x) only when specific clinical criteria are met. Plain radiography (X-ray) is the first-line imaging study for knee osteoarthritis and is sufficient in most cases.

## Indications considered medically necessary

MRI of the knee may be appropriate when **at least one** of the following criteria is met:

1. **Suspected internal derangement** beyond osteoarthritis: meniscal tear, ligamentous injury, or loose body, where the diagnosis cannot be confirmed by physical examination and plain radiographs.
2. **Failed conservative therapy:** the patient has completed at least 6 weeks of documented conservative management (NSAIDs, physical therapy, activity modification) without meaningful improvement, AND surgical intervention is being considered.
3. **Pre-surgical planning** for total or partial knee arthroplasty when plain films are inconclusive about the extent of cartilage loss or subchondral bone involvement.
4. **Suspected osteonecrosis or stress fracture** that is not visible on plain radiographs.
5. **Acute trauma** with mechanical symptoms (locking, giving way, inability to bear weight) and inconclusive plain films.

## Indications generally NOT considered medically necessary

- Initial workup of knee pain without prior plain radiographs
- Routine evaluation of established osteoarthritis without a change in symptoms
- Patient request without a documented clinical indication
- Screening of asymptomatic individuals

## Required documentation

For approval, the medical record must contain:

- A documented diagnosis with specific ICD-10 code
- Results of prior plain radiography of the affected knee within the past 12 months
- Documentation of conservative therapy attempted, with dates and outcomes
- A clinical indication that fits one of the medically necessary categories above
- The clinical question the MRI is intended to answer

## Notes

This guideline is consistent with American College of Radiology (ACR) Appropriateness Criteria for chronic knee pain. Rules may vary by health plan; refer to plan-specific medical policy for coverage details.
